Chen Group

Electrochemistry & Semiconductor Physics Lab

We explore how ions and electrons behave in materials — from batteries that store more energy to semiconductors that push electronics further. Our group combines advanced characterizations, materials syntheses, and theoretical modeling to understand charge carrier behavior in electrochemical and semiconductor materials.

Research

We study how ions and electrons move, react, and are stored in solids, and apply this understanding to batteries, semiconductor devices, and chip manufacturing. Our approach combines synthesis and fabrication, experimental characterization, and theory and modeling.

Energy Storage

Lithium Batteries

Storage capacity and charging speed are governed by charge-carrier thermodynamics and kinetics in battery materials. We elucidate storage, transport, and degradation, develop electrochemical diagnostics, and design electrode materials and solid/polymer electrolytes for high capacity, fast charging, and long cycle life.

Electrode materialsSolid & polymer electrolytesDiagnosis
Electronics

Semiconductor Junctions

Semiconductor device characteristics are governed by how charge carriers are distributed at junctions and defects. We combine electrical and spectroscopic characterization with modeling to study junction electrostatics, carrier profiles, trap-state distributions, and defect reactions in power electronics materials.

Schottky and p-n junctionsDeep-level trapsCharge profiling
Fundamentals

Mixed Ion–Electron Conductors

Ionic and electronic carriers determine how solids store charge and react with their surroundings. We combine defect chemistry and modeling with electrochemical measurements to study carrier equilibria, defect–defect interactions, space charge effects, and coupled ion–electron transport under non-equilibrium conditions.

Solid-state ionicsDefect chemistrySpace chargeTransport
Electrochemical Processes

Electrochemistry in Semiconductor Manufacturing

Redox reactions at solid–liquid interfaces are central to semiconductor manufacturing. We apply electrochemical principles to (electro)chemical mechanical polishing and post‑CMP cleaning, and use redox potential control, guided by Pourbaix diagrams, to convert metal ions in fab wastewater into functional oxides for catalysis.

E-CMPPost-CMPMetal recoveryCatalysisInterfaces
Modeling & Computation

Power Device & Battery Design

We develop physics-based models that link materials properties to device performance, and use them to design device and electrode architectures. Current efforts include device structures for power semiconductor diodes and redox transistors, and the microstructure of composite electrodes for solid-state batteries.

Diode simulationComposite electrodesFinite element modeling
Advanced Characterizations

Imaging, Spectroscopy & Transport Analysis

We combine X-ray tomography, polarized optical microscopy, synchrotron spectroscopy, and transport measurements, including operando experiments, to analyze structure, ion transport, and degradation. AI-assisted image processing and 3D reconstruction turn these data into descriptors for materials design.

Operando characterizationX-ray tomographyAI image analysis

Chia-Chin Chen (陳嘉晉)
Chia-Chin Chen is an Associate Professor in the Department of Chemical Engineering at National Taiwan University. He received his B.S. in Chemical Engineering from National Taiwan University and his M.S. in Chemical Engineering from the Illinois Institute of Technology. He then earned his Ph.D. in Chemistry at the Max Planck Institute for Solid State Research in Germany. Before joining NTU, he was a postdoctoral fellow in the Department of Materials Science and Engineering at Stanford University.His honors include the award for the Best Dissertation in Solid State Chemistry and Materials Science in Germany, the Yushan Young Scholar Fellowship from the Ministry of Education, and the NSTC 2030 Cross-Generation Young Scholars Award (Emerging Young Scholar and Excellent Young Scholar).

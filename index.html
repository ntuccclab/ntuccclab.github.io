Chen Group
Electrochemistry & Semiconductor Physics Lab

We explore how ions and electrons behave in materials — from batteries that store more energy to semiconductors that push electronics further. Our group combines advanced characterizations, materials syntheses, and theoretical modeling to understand charge carrier behavior in electrochemical and semiconductor materials.

Research

We study the chemistry and physics of charge carriers in solids — ions and electrons in materials — bridging fundamental transport science and real-world materials design for energy and electronics.

Li-ion Batteries Semiconductor Physics MOF Electrodes Electro-chemo-mechanical Modeling
Energy Storage
Li-ion Batteries
Storage capacity and charging speed are governed by charge-carrier thermodynamics and kinetics in electrode materials. We elucidate storage and transport mechanisms, and design new materials with high capacity and fast charging rates — including solid electrolytes, space charge effects, and polymer electrolytes.
Electrode materialsTransport kineticsSolid electrolytesSpace chargePolymer electrolyte
Electronic Devices
Semiconductor Physics
Device properties are governed by lattice defects. We study defect-induced reactions, diffusion processes, and phase transitions — combining theoretical modeling, thin film fabrication, and spectroscopic characterization — to develop materials for power electronics and memory applications.
Defect physicsPhase transitionsThin filmSpin defectsChemical mechanical polishing
Theory & Computation
Electro-chemo-mechanical Modeling
We develop continuum models to understand charge carrier equilibrium, space charge effects, and transport at interfaces and in bulk mixed ionic–electronic conductors. Theory guides experimental design, and experimental results validate and refine the models.
Continuum theoryPhase-field modelingInterface thermodynamicsMixed conductors
Materials Design
MOFs & Composite Electrodes
Conductive metal-organic frameworks and nanocomposites offer tunable porosity and redox-active sites for simultaneous ionic and electronic transport. We design, synthesize, and characterize MOF-based electrodes for energy storage and pseudocapacitor applications.
MOF synthesisConductive MOFsPseudocapacitorsInterfacial storage

People

Chia-Chin Chen (陳嘉晉)
Associate Professor · Department of Chemical Engineering, NTU
Prof. Chen studies the electrochemistry and physics of conductive materials, focusing on charge carrier behavior in energy storage and semiconductor systems. He received his PhD from Stuttgart University / Max Planck Institute for Solid State Research, and conducted postdoctoral research at Stanford University (Chueh Group) before joining NTU in 2020.
